## Retail Fuel Pricing Dynamics

Petroleum products sit at the center of the Indian economy. Beyond personal mobility, they serve as critical operational inputs across freight logistics, power generation backups, agriculture, and manufacturing. Any upward revision in retail pump prices immediately feeds into transport freight, triggering second-round inflationary pressure across essential consumer goods. 

Compared to neighboring South Asian economies, Indian consumers pay a substantial premium at the pump, primarily due to the heavy incidence of Central Excise Duties and State Value Added Tax (VAT).

### The Policy Shift: From Regulation to Market Determination
For decades, retail fuel prices were administered by the central government under the **Administered Pricing Mechanism (APM)**. Deregulation took place in two stages:
* **Petrol:** Deregulated in June 2010.
* **Diesel:** Deregulated in October 2014.

Oil Marketing Companies (OMCs) initially transitioned to fortnightly price revisions linked to global crude benchmarks. In **June 2017**, India adopted a **Daily Dynamic Pricing Model**, designed to transmit changes in the 15-day rolling average of international benchmark prices incrementally. In practice, however, OMCs frequently freeze retail revisions during sharp global crude price spikes or domestic elections to mitigate margin shocks and cushion consumer impact.

### Structural Import Dependency vs. Refining Dominance
* **High Crude Import Reliance:** India imports roughly **88%** of its crude oil requirements (up from ~76% in 2000–01 and ~82% in 2016–17), driven by steady domestic demand growth alongside flat domestic crude extraction. Consequently, retail pricing remains acutely vulnerable to swings in the **Indian Crude Basket** and fluctuations in the **USD/INR exchange rate**.
* **Refining Capacity Surplus:** Despite crude import dependency, heavy domestic capital investment has made India a net exporter of refined petroleum products (such as high-speed diesel and aviation turbine fuel).

## The June 2020 Delhi Fuel Inversion

The secular compression of the price differential between petrol and diesel culminated in an unprecedented market inversion in mid-2020, when diesel traded at a premium to petrol in New Delhi for the first time on record.

### Key Factors Behind the Anomaly:
1. **The Price Inversion (June 24, 2020):**
   Retail diesel in Delhi reached **₹79.88 per litre**, surpassing petrol at **₹79.76 per litre**. Historically, diesel enjoyed an ₹18 to ₹20 per litre discount to support agricultural logistics and public transport.
2. **The Delhi VAT Restructuring:**
   The inversion was driven by sub-national fiscal policy rather than international crude movements. On **May 5, 2020**, following earlier central excise increases:
   * Petrol VAT was raised from 27% to **30%** (+₹1.67/L).
   * Diesel VAT was sharply hiked from 16.75% to **30%** (+₹7.10/L in a single move).
3. **Tax Share at the Pump:**
   As international crude prices plunged to historic lows during early pandemic lockdowns, retail price cuts were offset by duty increases. Combined Central and State taxes accounted for approximately **64% of the retail selling price** during this period.
4. **Localized Market Impact:**
   The price inversion remained confined to the National Capital Territory of Delhi. In major metros such as Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai, diesel maintained a discount of **₹3.50 to ₹9.50 per litre** over petrol because state governments maintained differentiated VAT structures for both fuels.
